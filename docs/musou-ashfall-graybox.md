# Ashfall Citadel graybox (Musou Mode's battlefield, G1)

Status: done on the branch (generator, server, content, client view, tool, tests; see Results), awaiting the owner's playtest. On branch `musou/ashfall-graybox`. Owner's start: 2026-11-04 (agent-coordination `decisions/2026-11-04-ashfall-graybox-start.md`).
Merge gate: the owner playtests a branch build. Everything here is **ours** (placeholder demonstration) unless it says otherwise; every number is an estimate
for the owner's playtest and goes to the refine register.

Design sources: the owner's overhead reference image of 2026-11-04 (a 3 x 3 block city, not to scale), the living spec
(agent-coordination `specifications/musou-mode/ashfall-citadel.md`, change register MUS-001..014) and the kit standard
(https://claude.ai/artifact/7kAJ5yEvwQwVK6kt6LumbS: 4 m module, 6 m storey, measured movement values).

## What changes
- Musou Mode's battlefield becomes Ashfall Citadel, a level generated in code (primitive geometry only, no install files, no art).
  Manufactorum stays for classic PvP, untouched. The Stormbird hub and the Firing Range are composed beside it as today (`TfwMap.Compose`).
- The war's rules, nodes, enemies, commander and HUD are unchanged: the generated level supplies what Manufactorum supplied
  (collision, three capture points A/B/C, a start area for each side, spawn markers that seed the enemies' graph).
- Restore switch: the old composition stays reachable as map `tfw_war_m09` (Manufactorum + Stormbird + range, `TfwMap.Version` 7 behaviour);
  `tfw_war` is Ashfall. Both names need `--content musou`. The client follows the map name it is sent.
- No boss gate, no new progression rules (separate proposal later). The commander stands in the Chaos start area, which is now the stronghold's courtyard.

## Frame and units
Simulation frame: X east, Y north, Z up, metres. Map centre (0, 0), ground at z = 0. Kit standard: horizontal positions on the 4 m grid (1 m for trim,
0.5 m for rubble), every walkable top on a 0.5 m level, storeys 6 m (walkable at 0, 6, 12), structural walls 1 m thick (graybox: up to 4 m for ramparts).

## Geometry rules for the generator
- Primitives: axis-aligned box, oriented box (yaw, optional pitch), box-step stair (risers 0.25 m), heightfield ground (2 m cells over the playable
  square, flat 0 except craters and rims). Every solid's triangles wind outward (as `TfwMap.Triangles`).
- Each primitive carries a kind for drawing: Ground, Road, Paving, Curb, Wall, Cover, Rubble, Floor, Stair, Perch (jump-only tops), Objective (a floor
  marker disc, not solid), Stronghold, Boundary, Backdrop.
- Collision: character mesh = every solid; weapon mesh = the same triangles. No kill volumes. No props.
- Budgets (kit standard, section 6): whole level under 200k collision triangles; report the count.
- Deterministic: the same triangles in the same order on every machine; the level hash is an FNV over the vertices and a generator version.

## Checks (automated, no bot gameplay)
1. Determinism: two builds give the same hash; the hash changes with the generator version.
2. Navigation (the enemies' graph builder, `NavGraph.Build` with the player's settings): on foot from the Marine start area, A, B, C, the courtyard, the
   plaza corners, and every ground-access upper floor (A plinth top, C first floor, E ruin first floor, small ruin floor, bunker roof, artillery barrel top, wall tops of the L walls) are one connected graph; the jump-only perches (obelisks, arch top, ruin roofs, keep top) are not reached on foot. Report the build time and memory.
3. No spawn marker, start area centre or capture centre inside a solid; each stands on walkable ground.
4. Triangle counts per kind and total under budget.
5. Isolation: PvP config hashes (faithful and extended) and the Manufactorum map hash unchanged; `tfw_war_m09` composes exactly as `TfwMap` version 7 did.
6. A top-down plan rendered from the generator (`smtool ashfall-plan --out <file.svg>`): every solid by kind, the grid, the objectives, start areas, Stormbird and range, a 50 m scale bar.

## Known gaps and risks
- The enemies' graph and flow field over about 5 times Manufactorum's area: build time, cache size and crowd cost are measured, not known.
- The Musou wire box (positions quantised within a box) grows with the map: check its precision.
- Square box steps and box rubble give a rough sense of the final ruins only.
- Lighting and sky: the client's provisional look (warm sun, ash-grey fog); no map effects or ambient sound on the generated level.

## Results (generator, server, content, tool and tests)
Generator `AshfallMap.Build` (version 1 here; see Pass 2 below for version 2; `AshfallMap.Build.cs` holds the layout, `AshfallBuilder.cs` the primitives), all ours:
- **Triangles** (collision, character mesh = weapon mesh): total 16,940 of the 200,000 budget, in 569 parts. Per kind: Ground 10,208 (5 parts: four slabs and the crater heightfield, 2 m cells over the west-middle block, with skirts), Road 36, Paving 12,
  Curb 96, Wall 684, Cover 192, Rubble 2,556 (213 pieces), Floor 108, Stair 2,700 (225 box steps), Perch 60, Stronghold 48, Boundary 48, Backdrop 192; the three objective discs (not solid) add 72.
  `smtool ashfall-plan` prints the same table.
- **Enemies' graph** (`NavGraph.Build` as the server builds it, `NavSettings.From` of a normal `SimConfig`, the war's walking area: the level's markers and the Firing Range's seed): 800,782 nodes; **cold build about 56 s** on the
  development machine (Manufactorum's much smaller graph takes seconds), about 67 MiB retained, **cache file about 68 MiB**, loaded from the cache in 0.3 s. So a server or a client test aid builds it once per machine and cache folder; the
  first start of a new server copy (a fresh VM) waits about a minute. The test is marked `[Trait("Speed", "Slow")]` and takes about a minute.
- **Wire box** (`TfwUseWireBox`; 16 bits an axis over the graph's box widened by the Stormbird and the range): span about 660 x 583 x 57 m, so a step of 1.01 / 0.89 / 0.09 cm and a worst position error of about 0.5 cm. Well under 5 cm: no change needed.
- **Checks, all automated** (tests/SmNet.Tests/Extras/Musou/Ashfall): two builds bit-equal and the hash follows the version; every box winds outward; spawns, start areas and capture centres stand on walkable ground outside every solid; budgets;
  rubble 0.35 m or 0.9..2.5 m high with gaps 0 or at least 1.2 m (separated pairs measured on the footprints); the graph connects Marine start, A, B, C, the courtyard, the plaza corners, A's plinth, the C, E-ruin, small-ruin and bunker floors,
  the artillery platform and barrel top, both L walls' tops (ramps) and the small monument's plinth on foot, and reaches none of the six jump-only perches (A and small obelisks, arch top, keep top, C and E roofs); the classic config
  hashes are pinned; `TfwMap.WithHub(level, manufactorum: true)` keeps the old pinned hash and `tfw_war_m09` composes as version 7 did.

Deviations from the design, with reasons:
- **Statue** centred (12, 8), not (6, 4): at (6, 4) with a 30 degree turn the box covers B's centre.
- **A's centre is at z = 3** (the plinth top; its zone, half height 8, still covers the ground); the A disc is drawn on the ground, under the plinth. The other points and the start areas stand at the block surface (0.25 m; the plaza 0.27 m).
- **Obelisk** at the plinth's north-west part, not its middle, so A's centre is open ground.
- **Road and paving** are 2 cm overlays (kind Road, Paving) above the surface, not flush, so they never share a plane with the ground (no z-fighting; a 2 cm lip is walked over).
- **Stairs inset**: a stair's boxes stop 0.25 m inside its nominal width and its treads start 0.25 m early (the last step still meets the platform). The graph samples a 0.5 m grid, and where a box edge lies exactly on a grid line it found the
  ground under the edge instead of the step, cutting stairs off from the ground beside them (found by the graph check; the L-wall ramps and two others were unreachable). Edges between grid lines walk. The graph builder itself is unchanged.
- **Small ruin and bunker stairs**: the small ruin's stair runs outside along its south wall to a doorway whose sill is at 6 m (24 steps do not fit in 10 m inside); the bunker's runs inside its open west half to the roof.
- **C ruin**: two straight flights and a landing, then a connector slab to the north half's floor; the flights end where the floor begins (a thin slab over the climb would block it).
- **Walls and pieces on the grid**: ruins' breaches are made from box pieces (`HoleWall`), so a breach's sill is solid.
- **Rubble** is placed by a fixed integer hash on the 0.5 m grid with yaw in 15 degree steps, keeping 1.2 m from every other piece, structure and marker; a cluster may place fewer pieces than its count. Hand-placed low walls and collapsed pieces between the
  craters are kept only on flat ground: in version 1 two of the eleven (the low wall at (-110, 40) and the collapsed piece at (-176, 5)) fall on a rim or a slope and are dropped, so the crater field has five low walls and four collapsed pieces.
- **Hash** is FNV over the solid corners' bits salted with `AshfallMap.Version` (as designed); the map hash the handshake compares (`TfwMap.Version` 8) is the composed world's.
- **Restore switch**: `TfwMap.ManufactorumName` (`tfw_war_m09`, `--map tfw_war_m09`); `TfwMap.WithHub(level, manufactorum)` has no one-argument form any more (callers state which battlefield); `TfwMap.Compose` takes `manufactorum` as a last optional argument (default true, the old behaviour).
- `smtool ashfall-plan` draws every solid by kind (shade: higher is darker; F/P labels with the top height on upper floors and perches), the grid, objectives with radii, both start areas, the Stormbird and the range, a scale bar and a north arrow. Not yet looked at by a person.

Unfinished or open: the first cold start of a server builds the graph for about a minute (a shipped or pre-built cache is a later decision); the plan SVG and the whole layout await the owner's eyes; no nav jump links (`.navjump`) were measured.

### Skins (over graybox boxes)
A part is read as a box (`AshfallBox.Of`): 12 triangles, flat top and bottom, any 15 degree yaw. Anything else (heightfield ground, convex roofs, the pitched barrel, objective discs) gets no skin. Local X is the box's longer horizontal side. A box is tiled exactly (N copies along the length, rows stacked in height), so every item lies inside the part's box (the checks allow 0.15 m, the draft uses 0): what is seen is what is hit. The tiling is the fewest items that keep the three scale factors within 1:2.2 of each other (else 1:3); a model that cannot do that is not used. The model is chosen among the candidates by triangle cost (items x the model's triangles): any candidate within twice the cheapest is eligible and the part's hash picks. A part that has skins is hidden (`HiddenParts`): the tiles cover its four faces and its top, and the openings (windows, doorways, gateways) are separate boxes in the graybox, so they stay open.

## Kit look (client)
The client draws Ashfall Citadel in two looks of the same map `tfw_war`: the graybox (`AshfallView`, unchanged) and the kit look (`AshfallKitView`), which draws `AshfallDressing.Items` with the owner's generated models and leaves out the graybox parts in `HiddenParts`; the remaining graybox parts get a stone and ash material (no checker); the objective discs and letters stay. Both are built once and switched by visibility: **F8** in game, or `--ashfall-look kit|graybox` at start (default kit when `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sm-reimpl\ashfall-kit\source\` exists, else graybox). The models are read at runtime from that private folder (`AshfallKitLibrary`: Godot `GltfDocument`, no editor import, on 3 background threads, one cached mesh per asset id, LODs from `ImporterMesh.GenerateLods`, textures shrunk to 1024 px and S3TC-compressed); nothing is copied into the repository or a build. A missing folder or file logs one line and that item stays graybox (a hidden part stays drawn until every Skin item over it is shown). Fitting follows the `AshfallDressItem` rule; the axis mapping is a rotation, not a mirror: glTF X to local X, glTF -Z to local Y (the model's front, glTF +Z, faces south at yaw 0), glTF Y to Z; swapped: glTF Z to X, glTF X to Y. Measured on the development PC (12 threads, Forward+): all 40 models load in about 6 s of background time (3 workers, 17.7 s summed; 3.2 s with `--ashfall-tex 0`), about 85 MiB of textures; items appear as their model arrives. Per-instance `MeshInstance3D` with mesh LODs is used (a MultiMesh has no culling or per-instance LOD, and a synthetic dense dressing of 2,591 items would put 16 M triangles on screen). The kit look sets the viewport's mesh LOD threshold to 4 (engine default 1; `--ashfall-lod-threshold`): on the dense synthetic map that cut GPU time from 20-28 ms to 2-5 ms per frame. Other aids: `--ashfall-tex N`, `--ashfall-lods off`, `--ashfall-shadows off`. Test aid `godot.cmd --windowed -- --ashfall-kit-gallery [--shots all|overview|AC-001,..] [--out dir]` lays out all 40 models in labelled boxes (previews in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sm-reimpl\cache\previews\ashfall-kit\`); with `--ashfall-dress` it shows the map with the real dressing, with `--ashfall-dense` a synthetic dressing (a Skin item over every Wall, Cover and Rubble part, test aid only) and prints KITPERF lines (frame time, draw calls, primitives, both looks). **Placeholder demonstration** (draft assets, to compare by eye in the owner's playtest).

### Pipeline
1. `AshfallMap.BuildGraybox()` = Ashfall v5 exactly (version 5, hash `0xAE096E323BB5A4D3`, pinned): the reference. The generator tags each part with a **structure group** while it builds

   its structure's bounds grown by 1 m (never into lanes, the gate approach, capture zones plus 6 m, start areas, spawn markers), shrinking rather than growing. When no asset fits within the

   AC-023, AC-020; landmarks: AC-027, AC-028, AC-029, AC-031, AC-032, AC-013, AC-002 (arch), AC-030 (wreck); backdrop blocks (outside the boundary): AC-010, AC-005, AC-030.

   middle so a lane stays open. The gate x -12..12 is one AC-006 fortified gate segment. The keep is one asset (AC-030 or AC-005 by fit, flagged: no keep asset), the keep tower one AC-010,
   the stair towers one AC-007 or AC-010 each, the bridge one AC-009 if it fits between what stands at its ends (else dropped and flagged), the outerwork terraces AC-008 each, the east outer

   ruin floors, platform decks, bridges, ramparts, the keep roof, terraces) as kept, changed or lost.
7. **Repeated surfaces** (`AshfallMap.VariationSeed`, default 1, a constant; `--ashfall-seed` test aid if cheap): road and plaza tiles (Surface items) turn only where their seams and

## Draft 6: collision quality gate
Owner's playtest note (2026-11-06) on AC-010, the tower lookout stump: "poor collision mapping on this object. ensure this doesn't happen with future additional assets." Everything here is ours (thresholds an estimate, to be checked by eye in the Shift+F8 overlay and in a playtest).

**The rule for new assets.** Every kit model gets its collision from `smtool ashfall-kit-collision` and must pass the gate. The command fits each model with every setting of `AshfallKitFit.GateLadder` and keeps the passing fit with the fewest boxes; it prints each model's measures (and the measures of the fit committed before the run), writes them into the report and into `AshfallKitCollision.g.cs` (`AshfallKitCollision.Quality(id)`, `Settings(id)`), and exits with code 3 when a used model fails. `AshfallKitCollisionGateTests.Every_used_model_s_committed_collision_passes_the_gate` fails the build's tests for any used model whose committed measures fail, so a model added to `AshfallKit.Models` without a good fit cannot slip in. Only the models the manifest flags as never used (AC-015, AC-016, AC-026, `AshfallKitFit.Gate.ReportOnlyIds`) are reported without failing. When a new model fails: look at its `--views` PNG, then refine the model's geometry, add a setting to the ladder, or hand-make its boxes; do not raise the thresholds for one model.

**How it measures** (`AshfallKitFit.Measure`, the thresholds in one place, `AshfallKitFit.Gate`). The mesh is voxelised again at 144 cells on its longest side (3 x the finest fit grid), cracks up to 4 cells wide sealed first (the generated models are not watertight at that cell; real openings stay open), the solid is everything the outside cannot reach. Air within 3 fine cells of the solid (one cell of the 48-cell fit) is not counted. Measures, each with its limit:
- far air: empty cells inside the boxes, beyond that band, at most 8 % of the boxes' volume; overhang air (far air with solid above it: under a balcony, lintel or deck) at most 3 %; the worst single box at most 35 % far air;
- air pocket: the largest connected chunk of deep air (more than 6 fine cells from the model) inside the boxes, as the edge of a cube of its volume, at most 4 % of the longest side; deep air view: the share of the model's silhouette, seen along each axis, behind which a box holds deep air, at most 10 %;
- phantom view: box silhouette where the model is see-through (a filled doorway, window or open frame), at most 2 % of the model's silhouette along the worst axis; holes: model silhouette where the boxes are see-through (a hole through the collision), at most 2 %; missed: surface cells of the model farther than the band from every box, at most 5 % (a hollow fit of a solid model misses nothing a player can reach).
- reported only: the fine IoU (it punishes hollow fits of solid models, which are harmless).

**What the fit changed** (`AshfallKitFit.Fit`). Dust is now a loose piece of solid (a 26-connected piece under 0.2 % of the solid), removed before merging. Draft 5 dropped every one-cell-thick merged box under that size; at the walkable models' 48 cells that cut thin walls into strips and dropped them: AC-010 lost 28 % of its solid, AC-003 15 %, AC-005 18 %, which left holes through their walls (the gate's holes and missed). A merge in `Reduce` is still limited by the fill tolerance, but an opening blocks it only where the opening is more than one cell from the solid (the old rule treated every gap of the surface's own cell as a doorway and kept 48-cell fits far over their caps). An optional seal (`seal`, one cell) closes cracks before the interior is filled. The ladder: walkable models (`AshfallKitFit.WalkableIds`) stay at 48 cells (the placement's step rule reads their cell size) with caps 64 or 128; the others try 24, 32 and 48 cells with caps 32 or 64; fill tolerance 15, 10 or 5 %; seal 0 or 1.

### The overlap rule (`AshfallOverlap.cs`, `AshfallDraft7.cs`)
- **Measure.** Two different items' collision boxes (fitted to the models, so they stand for the drawn meshes) meet by their penetration depth: the least overlap along Z and the four horizontal axes of the two boxes (separating axes). Over `AshfallOverlap.Tolerance` = **0.3 m** is an overlap (the narrow-gap rule closes slots by 5 cm; fitted boxes stand up to a cell proud of the mesh). Ramp wedges are not boxes and are not measured.
- **Resolve, at placement** (after the draft-6 growth settles an item, before it is emitted; every earlier item stays): the later item moves out along the shallowest separating axis to within half the tolerance (they still touch: no slot opens), at most 4 m or a quarter of its longer side (stronghold pieces and terraces 8 m), never into a protected place it did not already touch, never adding a narrow gap under 2.4 m; else its footprint shrinks to 0.85 and 0.7 (stronghold pieces 0.55) and moves again; else the pool's next model is tried; else it is left out and logged ("dropped: overlap ...", with the asset and item it met). A stronghold leg wall keeps its place along the leg (no breach closes): it is trimmed at the end that meets the other piece (a corner tower), by at most a third of its length.
- **Exempt:** bridges (AC-045, AC-009): their ends rest on what carries them, an intended attachment (`AshfallMap.OverlapExempt`).
- **Numbers:** without the rule (`AshfallMap.RebuildWithoutOverlapRule()`, a report aid) the draft-6 placement has **156 overlapping pairs**, deepest 6.6 m (the keep into the keep tower). ### The rules (`AshfallDraft9.cs`, `AshfallStairs.cs`, `AshfallStairDetect.cs`)
- **Stairs from the meshes.** `smtool ashfall-kit-stairs` reads each walkable model's own mesh (owner's private library, read only) and writes `AshfallKitStairs.g.cs` (numbers only): the mesh's top surface on a 128-cell grid, read along rows and columns as plateaus; a stair is a chain of three or more rises from a floor through treads (each two cells deep or more, no riser over three times another) to a landing, on four or more neighbouring lines that end on the same landing. A smooth slope (a cheek, a rubble ramp) has no treads and is not a stair; a wall top one line wide is not a stair. A stair that stops under a deck (the expansion's medium platform AC-050 stops about four risers short of its deck) is carried up to the deck when the gap is at most six of its risers.
- **Stair ramps count as solid**: a ramp carried past its model must not meet what stands, and its box joins the narrow-gap and overlap registries for the items after it. The cut never deletes a box standing over a slice of the ramp (a beam, a deck): it stays whole and the walk check decides. Every turn uses the exact 15-degree table (the same bits on the Windows clients and the Linux server).
- **One ramp per stair, the steps cut under it.** In the level each stair becomes one ramp over its step nosings, from the floor at its foot to its landing (movement only, as before: shots meet the steps' boxes); the model's own boxes under the ramp are cut down below it, in up to three slices along the climb, so no riser pokes through; a stair whose first riser stands up to 1.5 m over the floor in front of it is carried down to that floor at its own slope. Kept only when it is 1 m wide or more, rises 0.8 m or more and is no steeper than 50 degrees after the placement's scale, and when on the item alone a walker climbs from 1.5 m in front of its foot onto its landing and back down past its foot without a stall; else no ramp and the boxes stay as fitted (named in the stair flag).
- **Openings at the foot, landings at the top.** Each kept stair keeps a clear approach of 1.5 m in front of its foot and a landing of 1.5 m beyond its top, the stair's width, from a step over the floor to head height (1.9 m). No other item's box may stand in them, and an item whose own stair zone meets a placed box yields: the draft-7 moves, shrinks, next model, else left out (`Conflict` in the overlap rule).
- **No stacked models.** Two placed models' drawn boxes (the whole model as the eye sees it) may meet by at most 0.3 m; the later one yields as above. Exempt: the bridges on their supports, and the stronghold's ring pieces among themselves (gate, corners, leg walls: one continuous wall line). Height comes from one scaled model per structure, as since draft 5.
- **Urban rules.** Buildings (ruin shells and corners, facades, towers, the bunker, walls over cover height) turn their front to the nearest street where turns score alike; built pieces stand square to the street grid; loose debris (rubble, collapsed wall pieces) is placed after the buildings and only within 8 m of a standing building's drawn box ("dropped: urban" otherwise). AC-049 (low firing platform) is no longer used where a walkable platform is wanted: its stair stops about 1.8 m under its deck, a stair that ends in its own wall.

### Stairs (the owner's first rule)
- **The defect in draft 10.** The two-storey ruin AC-051 stands on a 1.1 m plinth, and its flight to the upper floor starts on that plinth.
  - After the scale, its door steps rise 0.29 m and then 0.57 m, more than the player's 0.4 m step.
  - The stair finder treated those steps as "a few steps, no ramp".
  - Draft 10's access rule counted any foot within 2 m of the base as ground.
  - Result: on draft 10, **26 flights outside the stronghold cannot be reached on foot**, and no player can get into the ruin at all.
- **Entry ramps (city only).** A flight whose foot stands on a raised floor gets an entry ramp at 25 degrees from its door down to the street (`EntryRamp` in `AshfallDraft12.cs`).
  - It is built as one of the model's stairs: the model's boxes are cut under it, its foot is cleared and zoned, and it is walked on the item.
  - The ramp must stay off the roadways.
  - Where no entry ramp fits, the flight gets a "foot stands" note. Draft 10's stair-access rule then replaces the model with the next one in its pool, as the owner asked on 2026-11-09.
  - After the stair pass below, 15 model choices were replaced, and every kept AC-051 has its entry ramp.
- **The final check** (`AshfallStairAccess`) runs on the final collision of the whole level, for any layout. For every stair:
  - its foot and its landing are open;
  - a ground foot is reached from the streets: the grid's walkable ground joined to the Imperial assembly area, then the player's capsule walked with the game's movement from the last open ground (1 m clearance) to the foot;
  - a raised foot is reached by walking from the ground floor behind it, or from a reached flight's landing;
  - the flight itself is walked up and down.
- **Enforcement.** The city's pass enforces the check (`EnforceStairAccess`), round after round: for each failing stair it removes the item in the way, or else the item that holds the stair. This run needed no removals.
- **Authored pieces** (the stronghold, the compound, the bridges) are checked and reported, never changed. The keep tower's top flight fails, as it does on drafts 10 and 11.

### Army routing on the avenues (the owner's decision, 2026-11-09)
The owner answered "Yes, route on avenues" when asked whether the army AI should keep its formations to the avenues. The armies' routes between the battle's places used to be one body's shortest walk on the walking graph (`NavGraph.FindPath`). On the city that walk cut diagonally through the blocks' 2 to 4 m alleys to A and C.

They are now searched with `NavGraph.FindPathWide` (`TfwRules.Version` 58):
- **Clearance.** Each node's clearance is the walk to the nearest node at a wall or an edge, built once per graph.
- **Cost.** A link whose narrower node has less than `TfwArmyRules.RouteHalfWidth` (10 m, a 20 m front) costs up to 21 times more.
- **Shortcuts.** The string pulling keeps a shortcut only where the line stays as wide as the cells it replaces.
- **Who uses it.** Formations (Ork waves, Guard armies and reinforcements) march the avenues and take a narrow way only where none wider is near: the stronghold's gate and its breaches, which are authored. Single bodies keep the shortest walk.
- **What is untouched.** Classic PvP's pathing, the graph's build and its cache.
